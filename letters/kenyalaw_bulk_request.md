I am a student researcher at [Institution], working on LexHack, a non-commercial research tool for studying Kenyan statutes and judgments. I am writing to ask for bulk access to Kenya Law's data, as your Terms of Use invite ("Please email us if you would like to access our data in bulk").

**1. About the project**

LexHack studies how Kenyan legislation and case law interact over time. Our first research question is how judicial declarations of unconstitutionality are reflected in the statute book. Examples include section 29 of the Kenya Information and Communications Act (*Geoffrey Andare v Attorney General*, 2016), section 194 of the Penal Code (*Jacqueline Okuta v Attorney General*, 2017), and the mandatory death sentence under section 204 of the Penal Code (*Francis Karioko Muruatetu v Republic*, 2017). We want to trace these links systematically across the corpus, which cannot be done one document at a time through the website.

The project is [supervised by [Supervisor name, title] / part of [course, hackathon or programme]] and is not for commercial gain.

**2. Data requested**

In order of priority:

1. **Legislation:** the Laws of Kenya in structured form (Akoma Ntoso XML or HTML), with all point-in-time versions and editorial annotations.
2. **Case law:** judgments with their metadata (citation, court, case number, date, judges, parties), plus full text or the source documents (PDF, DOCX).
3. **Kenya Gazette:** issues with metadata, if available.

We are happy to take whatever format suits you best: a database or file export, access to your APIs with a research token, or a one-off data transfer. We would also welcome periodic updates, or guidance on how to pick up changes.

**3. Undertakings**

If access is granted, we undertake to:

- use the data only for non-commercial research, in line with the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 licence in your Terms of Use;
- credit Kenya Law as the source in all outputs, and link back to the authoritative version on kenyalaw.org;
- not redistribute the bulk dataset, and not make it publicly available as a substitute for Kenya Law's services;
- respect any extra conditions attached to particular collections, such as anonymised or sensitive judgments;
- share our findings and any errors we find in the data with Kenya Law, if that is useful to you;
- delete or return the data at the end of the project if you ask us to.

Having read your Terms of Use, we have not crawled your website. Apart from reading your robots.txt and Terms of Use, our only contact with it has been ordinary browsing. To understand the structure of your documents, we ran a small pilot (about 250 pages) on copies already held by the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. For anything at scale, we would much rather obtain the data from you directly.
